# Australian compliance notes for websites

Not legal advice - flag issues as risks for the client to confirm with their adviser.

## Everyone
- **Australian Consumer Law (ACL):** no misleading or deceptive claims. "Best in Melbourne",
  "cheapest", "guaranteed" need substance. "From $X" prices must be genuinely available. Guarantees
  must state their terms. Fake or cherry-picked reviews are a risk (ACCC has acted on this).
- **Privacy:** if the site collects personal info (forms, bookings, tracking pixels) it should have a
  privacy policy that matches what it actually collects and who it shares with. Businesses over
  AUD 3m turnover and all health service providers are covered by the Privacy Act / APPs.
- **Spam Act:** forms that add people to email/SMS marketing need consent and an unsubscribe path.
- **Accessibility:** the Disability Discrimination Act applies to websites; WCAG 2.x AA is the
  usual benchmark.
- **Business identity:** show the legal/trading name and ABN where invoices or quotes are issued.

## Trades
- Licence numbers: many states expect licence numbers on advertising for electricians, plumbers,
  builders and some other trades (rules vary by state - in Victoria check VBA/ESV requirements).
  Flag missing licence numbers as `[TO CONFIRM]` rather than asserting a breach.
- Warranty and "workmanship guarantee" claims must reflect actual terms.

## Health (AHPRA-regulated: physio, chiro, psych, dentists, OT, etc.)
- No testimonials about clinical aspects of care on the practitioner's own site/social.
- No misleading claims, no creating unreasonable expectations of benefit, no encouraging
  indiscriminate use of services, no offers/gifts without clear terms.
- Titles: use protected titles correctly; "specialist" only if registered as one.
- Before/after imagery for health is high risk - flag it.
- Pricing and gap information should be clear.

## NDIS providers
- Registered vs unregistered status stated accurately; no implying NDIA endorsement.

When a variation or report touches any of these, add a short `Compliance note` and keep claims
conservative.
